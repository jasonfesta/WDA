# Bunny Royale — Product

## The experience

Bunny Royale is a compact, funny third-person shooter whose first successful moment is easy to describe: a rabbit launches off a bounce pad, fires an absurd blaster across a suburban street, lands behind an enemy and keeps moving. Readable movement, character comedy and satisfying impacts matter more than a huge inventory or a sprawling map.

The intended audience is people who recognize the visual language of polished action games and want an immediately understandable session. A first session should establish one character, one weapon and one ability before introducing enemy variety. A proposed first-level duration of six to ten minutes is a design target, not a measured result.

## Core loop

Choose a fighter → inspect that fighter's loadout → enter Carrot Cove → clear three encounters → earn a result → retry with another fighter. The selection screen explains differences through a short action demonstration and a readable silhouette. It does not require reading an RPG stat sheet.

Pip rewards mobility with the Carrot Carbine and Double Hop. Bruno controls groups with the Seed Thumper and Ground Slam. Volt uses the Arc Fork and a Decoy Drone to create openings. These are distinct play styles, not a claim that one is objectively strongest. Numerical balance is deliberately deferred until movement and feedback can be tested.

The first level is a wave-based solo arena. Player-versus-player networking, a shrinking storm, construction systems, monetization and a live-service economy are outside this concept's first scope. They are not implied by the Fortnite reference. The appeal should survive without those systems.

## Design priorities

- The world is visible while playing. Health, ammo and objectives live at the edges; the aiming corridor remains open.
- An enemy's role is recognizable from shape, equipment and motion, including when colors are difficult to distinguish.
- A miss remains readable and funny. Failed landings, a harmless carrot splash or a briefly stunned rabbit can communicate failure without realistic injury.
- Selection, briefing and settings are deliberate separate states. They do not become permanent panels over the arena.
- The same character remains recognizable in turnaround, menu, HUD portrait and in-world scale.

## Decisions and open questions

Characters are approved; the revised neighborhood and menus are still a review proposal. Desktop is the first gameplay composition to study. Touch adaptation needs a separate control-layout pass rather than shrinking a desktop HUD. Controller support is a future design requirement, not an implemented feature.

Before any implementation authorization, settle the level's tone, desired session length, movement speed, how much cover can break and whether abilities recharge by time or by successful play. The current package supports those decisions with raster art and a detailed first-level scenario.
