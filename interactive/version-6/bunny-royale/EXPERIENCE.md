# Bunny Royale — Experience

## First minute

The opening menu places Pip inside the actual suburban world, with the logo and one clear Play action. The player hears and sees the same world they are about to enter; a future implementation should avoid a cinematic that suggests a different level. Settings and accessibility remain visible but secondary.

Play opens a dedicated character stage. Three portraits sit along the bottom. Selecting Pip, Bruno or Volt changes the central full-body presentation, weapon and one-line ability description. The environment stays behind the character, while a compact loadout label occupies an edge. Confirm opens a brief route preview: neighborhood, market, service yard. Back returns to the previous selection without resetting it.

The first playable area contains no immediate incoming fire. The player learns moving, looking, jumping and firing at a visible target. Short prompts disappear after their action is demonstrated and can be recalled from pause. Pip's Double Hop is introduced with a low fence; Bruno and Volt receive equivalent opportunities for their abilities. These are planned tutorials, not existing playable sequences.

## Carrot Cove sequence

| Encounter | Spatial idea | Lesson | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Neighborhood arrival | Blue house, street, low fence and side garden | Move between cover; identify a ranged raider | Clear a small introductory group |
| Market intersection | Red-brick frontage, parked truck, crates and rooftop route | Combine an ability with a flank; spot approaching hoppers | Clear two readable waves |
| Service yard | Open center, perimeter cover and a bounce-pad route | Read the foreman's windup; relocate before impact | Defeat the foreman and remaining attackers |

Spawn cues are visible and audible. New enemies enter from authored approaches rather than appearing directly behind the player without warning. The map allows recovery routes and avoids a single narrow choke point that makes one weapon mandatory.

## The memorable moment

At the market, a bounce pad carries the player over a parked pickup. The camera gives enough forward view to understand the landing. A short projectile trail shows the shot crossing the street. On touchdown, the player can flank the foreman's escort through the service yard. The moment uses existing environment and effect directions; it does not require a new cinematic system.

## Results and replay

Victory celebrates the selected rabbit with a readable summary: encounters completed, accuracy and a small number of meaningful accomplishments. These proposed values are not fabricated personal statistics. Retry preserves settings and returns to the level; Change Rabbit returns to selection. Defeat explains the immediate cause where possible, offers retry and never replaces the screen with a store.

Pause must genuinely suspend a future solo session. Focus loss should pause and require an explicit resume countdown. Returning from a menu should never release an accidental shot. These transitions are part of the experience, even though the current deliverable is art and documentation only.
