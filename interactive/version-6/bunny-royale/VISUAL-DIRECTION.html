Bunny Royale — Visual direction

World and rendering

Use the suburban environment V2 as the current world reference. It establishes blue wooden siding, a red-brick market, asphalt, a yellow car, a blue pickup, low wooden fences, bright grass and chunky trees under a blue-violet sky. Broad forms and simplified matte surfaces should read before tiny texture detail.

The earlier Mediterranean resort, tiled roofs, piazza and carrot tower are superseded studies. They must not re-enter a current contact sheet merely because their images are available. The desired connection to Fortnite is visual: familiar third-person scale, simplified architecture, saturated terrain and clean spatial hierarchy. The project retains its own rabbit cast and name.

Lighting is sunny and directional without swallowing opponents in black shadows. Muzzle flashes, impacts and ability effects use brief accents; persistent glow must not obscure targets. Stylized materials separate fur, padded armor, painted metal and translucent energy. A future renderer needs a material bible because the PNGs alone do not define physically consistent shading.

Preserve relative body mass, ear profile, backpack and weapon proportions across views. Expressions are exaggerated and mischievous. Playable fighters must remain distinct from the white-fur orange-vest raider, yellow-helmet hopper and blue-armored foreman.

Interface

The exact wordmark is BUNNY ROYALE. Large menu type is chunky and condensed; support labels remain plain and legible. White lettering, midnight-navy contrast and controlled teal/orange accents connect brand and HUD. Gameplay uses health/shield bottom left, ammunition and a short loadout bottom right, objective top center and a small crosshair.

Character selection can use a large hero figure because it is a separate state. Combat must not carry that selection panel into the world. Pause may intentionally cover the scene, but ordinary inventory and ability feedback stay short and temporary.

Motion and sound intent

Ears and armor sell anticipation, squash and recovery without blurring the hitbox. Each weapon needs a separate firing rhythm and impact sound; reuse a coherent material family rather than one generic explosion. Screen shake, flashes and camera kick need adjustable intensity. Motion, audio and rigging are future production directions; the current art does not contain those assets.
