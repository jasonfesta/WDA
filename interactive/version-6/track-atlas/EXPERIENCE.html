Experience

Proposed journeys, illustrated by the watercolor V2 screens. These interactions are not implemented.

The first three minutes

The opening view presents an Ink Score composition in a paused state. The track name, Play action and Open track action are immediately visible. One short invitation—“Play a track. Shape what you hear.”—explains the relationship between listening and making. No onboarding panel obscures the illustration.

The visitor presses Play. A restrained current and a small playhead identify the present musical moment. After several seconds, a quiet hint invites a drag on an illustrated phrase. The drag bends the phrase's composition while the audio continues unchanged. Releasing the pointer leaves a visible authored adjustment, with Undo available nearby.

The visitor marks two points in the transport to loop a short passage. The canvas revisits that passage consistently, allowing a useful comparison instead of inventing a wholly new picture on every replay. The visitor switches to Kinetic Poster at the same time position, arranges the title and saves a still. A confirmation identifies the saved mode and timestamp without blocking the canvas.

What each mode feels like

Ink Score unfolds as a painted river through hills and stems. A lower-frequency emphasis can widen the current; sharper events can place small ink marks. These are chosen artistic mappings. The listener can move a phrase's path and adjust the spacing of its marks. The underlying musical times stay fixed unless the listener explicitly changes the loop or seek position.

Kinetic Poster gives the track title a physical presence. Pigment pools along letter edges, with restrained changes in spacing or emphasis. The listener moves a word group, selects one of a few painted treatments and freezes a composition. Repositioning the title does not obscure the compact playback controls or make the words unreadable.

Listening Diary starts with fewer illustrated memories than the fully populated art study. Tapping Mark adds the current time and a small vignette. The listener chooses an annotation such as “pulse,” writes their own words, or leaves it unlabelled. Editing a diary entry reveals a temporary focused sheet; closing it returns to the same composition and audio position. A plain-text list of entries provides an equivalent way to review the session.

Returning and mobile use

A saved composition remembers the active mode, edits, notes, loop and motion preference. If the local audio is unavailable, show the composition immediately and explain how to reconnect it. Do not pretend playback is possible until a matching source is supplied.

Portrait mode recomposes the artwork vertically; it does not shrink a desktop canvas into a narrow column. Controls remain within reach and respect device safe areas. A tap selects a mark before a drag edits it, preventing accidental edits during navigation. Reduced-motion users receive deliberate still changes and the same editing capabilities. The reward remains authorship, even when continuous animation is disabled.
