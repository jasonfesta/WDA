Product definition — Magdalena Bay V2

Promise

BEATFRAME is a proposed three-lane rhythm experience set inside an artist's visual world. The selected direction is Magdalena Bay's surreal Imaginal Disk atmosphere, interpreted as a tactile 3D landscape around centered gameplay. The visitor should feel that each clean musical phrase draws them further into an unusual place. The game remains readable even when the scenery is expressive.

The concept uses the song label “Image.” Its presence in the artist's official catalog is verified by the official store tracklist. The title is a reference for the proposed experience; this package contains no recording, measured tempo, authored chart or proven playback. The artist selection is settled. Recording choice, chart design and difficulty remain later decisions.

Audience and first session

The primary visitor enjoys music, graphic design and short browser experiences. They may not be an expert rhythm player. Three obvious targets and a brief introductory phrase should make the interaction understandable before explanations are needed. Desktop uses D, F and J; mobile proposes direct taps on the three targets. The visitor can miss a note and recover without losing the experience or being pushed into a menu.

A returning visitor may want a cleaner run, a longer combo or a more demanding chart. Those goals should grow out of musical phrasing, not an endless increase in speed. The world is a reason to return, but accurate timing is the core activity. Environmental motion rewards successful passages while leaving the center lane corridor stable.

Product boundaries

This concept is separate from Jet Ski POV, Type Rave and track-analysis tools. It contains no steering, text composition, browsing dashboard or permanent settings sidebar. Song preparation, calibration and sound settings belong before play or in a temporary pause layer. A local-file practice mode is a possible later extension, not a substitute for a curated artist experience.

The current delivery is art and documentation only. It resolves the visual direction and screen hierarchy. It does not introduce new dependencies, game behavior, artist audio, cloud uploads, accounts or a live service. No playable prototype is included in this publication.

Success criteria and open choices

For concept review, a visitor should identify the three targets, read the artist and track, understand the score hierarchy and recognize a coherent dreamscape. The lanes must be centered on both formats. Later product decisions include chart authorship, difficulty range, whether hold notes belong in the first release, calibration design and motion limits. Functional success would require direct testing; the still images do not establish it.
