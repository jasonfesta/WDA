Proposed first minute

Compose

The opening scene is quiet: an oversized phrase on warm paper, generous negative space, and a small bottom strip. “Edit phrase” and “Play” are the emphasized actions. A visitor can understand that the words themselves are the material. There is no mandatory microphone prompt, account requirement, or list of technical parameters.

Editing opens a compact field near the lower edge. The visitor enters two to six words and chooses deliberate line breaks. The composition updates with stable baselines and useful margins. Typing must not trigger performance shortcuts. Exiting the field returns focus to the stage with the new phrase intact.

Perform

Play introduces the prepared loop and one obvious relationship: spacing expands and settles with a restrained pulse. The rest of the graphic stays stable so the visitor can see cause and effect. A short hint explains that dragging moves the composition's center or spread. It disappears after the visitor makes a visible adjustment.

Changing from Breathe to Stack keeps the words and palette. Complete lines begin moving as blocks rather than every glyph behaving independently. Elastic instead gives one selected word a brief stretch and a clear return. Preset changes should feel like different performances of the same phrase, not a destructive reset to unrelated sample content.

Find a frame

The visitor reduces intensity, waits for a strong arrangement, and presses Freeze. Motion and audio pause together by default. The stage now behaves like a poster: the phrase can be inspected, the frame can be saved, or performance can resume from the same moment. A freeze indicator should be unmistakable, because a still composition must not look like a stalled application.

Save would produce the selected graphic at a stated size in a later build. A project file could preserve phrase, line breaks, palette, behavior, and frozen state. The concept should not promise exact reconstruction of an unsaved live microphone performance.

Optional voice and return

Microphone is offered after the visitor understands the visual instrument. A deliberate enable action opens permission and level feedback. A clap or spoken sound can affect the already-entered phrase; speech transcription is not part of the proposed first scope. Silence, denial, or unavailable hardware leaves the prepared loop and pointer controls usable.

The first minute should finish with a personal phrase and at least one deliberate visual choice. The visitor can continue performing, revise the words, or leave with a still. No timeline editing or hidden property panel is necessary. These are experience intentions for review; no recording, microphone capture, or export has been implemented in this package.
