Product definition

The experience to preserve

Jet Ski POV is about reading moving water from the rider's position. The craft responds to the chosen line; a crest offers a jump; a controlled landing turns motion into satisfaction. The camera stays close to the handlebars so the body of the craft, the water surface, and the distant horizon communicate speed together. This direction was selected for its immediate physical appeal and should not be replaced with a disconnected dashboard or an abstract shader demo.

The intended visitor wants a quick escape into a vivid place. They may have no interest in vehicle simulation settings. A second audience enjoys mastering a compact course and improving clean landings. The concept should serve both through an approachable first run and optional experimentation, rather than by requiring different applications.

Proposed first scope

One original craft, one daylight environment, three course zones, and a small trick vocabulary are enough to establish the experience. A proposed three-minute course gives the session an understandable arc. Free ride removes the timer and gate obligation while keeping the same water and controls. The exact duration is a design target, not a delivered capability.

A first-time rider should accelerate, turn, cross a wake, make a simple jump, and recover from a rough landing without opening a manual. Tricks reward control but are not required to finish. A missed gate should prompt a useful direction cue rather than make the entire run unwinnable. A wipeout should cost the current unbanked chain and restore a clear starting pose.

Scope boundaries

This is separate from BEATFRAME. Music may provide atmosphere later, but score is based on riding actions and landing quality, not beat timing. Multiplayer, open-world travel, weather forecasting, branded vehicle replicas, deep customization, and realistic naval simulation are outside the initial concept.

The forward view is the product's main surface. Speed and boost belong on the craft; progress belongs at an edge. Detailed settings appear only before riding or in pause. A persistent control sidebar covering the sea is a failed interpretation.

Decisions before production

Reviewers should settle the balance between realism and playful exaggeration, how much the rider can rotate in the air, the default horizon stabilization, and the course's forgiving reset rules. Later success measures could include first-jump discovery and retry rate, but no user results are claimed. Current approval means agreement on the experience and its visual priorities, followed by a separate decision about whether to develop it.
