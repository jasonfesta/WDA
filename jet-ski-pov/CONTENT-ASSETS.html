Content and asset plan

Existing evidence

The delivered image is an existing first-person jet-ski photograph previously selected in the research. The local copy is references/jet-ski-pov-photograph.jpg. The recorded publisher is Uptown Jet Skis. The photograph shows handlebars, a rider's hand, open water, and a distant skyline. Its purpose is to communicate viewpoint and proportion; it is not an original course render or a working simulation screenshot.

Keep the original image credit attached when it appears in the review gallery. The photographer's individual name was not identified in the retained source record, so do not invent one. The final game's craft, coastline, and environment would require their own original asset treatment.

Proposed environment inventory

A future visual package should include a daylight sky, broad ocean material study, distant shoreline, one orientation landmark, buoy family, three gate configurations, a forgiving ramp, and a final jump feature. Art should be designed around the course's approach angles. A ramp that looks attractive in isolation but disappears against the horizon is not ready for use.

The craft package would require exterior model references, rider-view dashboard, grips, control labels, and optional hand poses. Material studies should distinguish wet plastic, rubber, metal, and readable display glass without excessive small detail. The first-person view takes priority over an unseen full vehicle customization set.

Effects and audio

Separate effect studies are needed for idle ripples, acceleration wake, turning spray, takeoff spray, clean landing, rough landing, and wipeout recovery. Every effect needs a duration and visibility rule. None should cover the landing target for most of the approach.

Proposed audio layers include engine load, surface contact, wind, impact, and a quiet confirmation cue. Music is optional atmosphere. It does not determine course timing or scoring. Audio and effects require provenance records and loudness review in any later production stage; no new sound library is included here.

Screens and handoff

Art planning should cover Ready, Cruise, Approach, Airborne, Landing, Pause, Recovery, and Results. Landscape phone and desktop should share a visual system but not identical control placement. An asset manifest should record intended role, dimensions, source, version, and approval status.

The current handoff is the reference plus detailed Markdown documents. There are no meshes, textures, shaders, executable level files, or finished game screens hidden behind the specification. Missing assets are explicitly future deliverables, allowing reviewers to approve scope before production begins.
