# Acceptance and review plan

## Accepting the current package

The current deliverable is complete when the preserved direction is clear, the reference is correctly labeled, and all specification documents are available. Reviewers should understand the difference between the existing photograph and proposed game visuals. The pack must not describe a playable simulation, completed physics, or measured performance as if those have been delivered.

The core concept should remain first-person riding with a low horizon, readable water, responsive steering, jumps, and controlled landings. It should remain separate from BEATFRAME. A reviewer should be able to identify the first-minute teaching sequence, the three course zones, and the difference between course play and free ride.

## Visual approval questions

Does the proposed camera preserve the relationship between grips, water, and horizon? Can the next crest or gate remain visible during spray? Is the course readable through world objects rather than a persistent map? Is the dashboard small enough to feel part of the craft? Does the touch proposal keep thumbs out of the central approach view?

The photographic reference should be assessed for framing only. Its skyline, craft branding, and real-world location are not automatic requirements. Any later concept render should be judged against the selected art direction rather than mistaken for a simulation capture.

## Interaction completeness

The documentation should explain how a rider starts, pauses, restarts, wipes out, recovers, and exits. Clean, rough, and wipeout outcomes must be distinguishable. A proposed trick action should have a clear availability condition. Recovery needs a reliable position, heading, and speed. A hidden tab should not imply uncontrolled travel.

Acceptance does not require inventing precise physics coefficients now. It does require identifying the decisions that influence comfort and trust: horizon stabilization, steering response, camera shake, airborne rotation, and the cost of a failed landing.

## Future implementation criteria

If development is separately authorized, later evidence should include one complete course run, a free-ride session, a repeatable wipeout recovery, and keyboard/touch comparisons. Tests should verify gate progression, single score banking, wave/contact alignment, pause integrity, and reset consistency. Performance reports must name device, browser, quality tier, and measured conditions.

Accessibility and comfort review should refer to [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) without claiming that a moving 3D experience is compliant based on screenshots. A reduced-motion setting should preserve navigation information. The forward canvas must stay free of bulky panels throughout ordinary riding. These remain future validation requirements, not completed test results.
