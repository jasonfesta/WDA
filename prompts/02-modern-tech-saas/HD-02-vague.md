# HD-02 — Vague Voice Prompt

## Brief

**Intent brief:** A startup wants a premium, modern tech/SaaS website with the polish of a leading tech brand.

**Category:** Editorial brand — tech/SaaS.

**Version:** Vague — broad brand direction and page structure. The detailed [explicit version](HD-02.md) retains the more specific research and art direction.

**Status:** In progress. Two voice entries captured.

## Sequence — how we developed the prompt

1. **Establish the intent:** a polished, premium modern tech website comparable to leading sites currently on the market.
2. **Define the broad visual language:** clean typography and backgrounds, with software presented above recurring gradient or dither textures.
3. **Set the brand range:** stark corporate through a more expressive middle to premium corporate, with Scale AI, Framer, and Resend as reference points.
4. **Establish the page flow:** sticky header, branded navigation, proposition and action, product tout, customer proof, features, product acquisition, newsletter, and footer.
5. **Specify navigation:** Platform, Resources, Developers, Pricing; Sign In and Start for Free or Try for Free on the right. Collapse the navigation for mobile.
6. **Connect palette and typography to the range:** move from traditional corporate SaaS through more expressive branding, with serif and characterful font explorations, then return to clean Inter- or SF Pro-like typography at the premium end.

## Final prompt — draft in progress

Create a premium, modern tech/SaaS website with a level of polish comparable to leading brands currently on the market. Keep the layout clean and typography-driven, with clean backgrounds and a clear presentation of the product.

Explore a range from **stark corporate → more expressive → premium corporate**. Use Scale AI as the corporate reference and Framer and Resend as references for a more distinctive, highly designed premium feel. Match the expression to the brand and its intended audience.

Carry a consistent texture language through heroes and product touts: gradients, gradation, or dithering, with the software presented clearly on top. Let this treatment connect the imagery throughout the brand.

Let the color palettes follow the same range, from traditional corporate SaaS through a more expressive middle to premium. Typography can progress through humanist serifs and more characterful display fonts, then return to restrained, clean sans-serif choices such as Inter or an SF Pro-like style at the premium end. Keep the choices coherent within each direction.

Build the page in this order:

1. Sticky header.
2. Logo and branded navigation across the top, collapsing for mobile.
3. Main headline, subheadline, and call to action.
4. Main product tout.
5. Customers the product serves.
6. Platform or product features, organized with horizontal tabs or a left-side tab navigation.
7. A clear way to get or start using the product.
8. Newsletter signup.
9. A comprehensive footer that repeats the main navigation and includes deeper links.

Use **Platform, Resources, Developers, and Pricing** as the main navigation groups. Place **Sign In** and **Start for Free** or **Try for Free** at the top right.

## Numbered voice trace

### Prompt 1

The vague version of this, I would say, is that we need to build a premium modern tech website that is polished and is equal to what is currently out there on the market. This presents itself in a couple of things:

- A very clean layout, typography-driven, with clean backgrounds.
- Image touts that have textures in them, whether it's gradation or a dither texture, that carry themselves through the brand. Wherever there are heroes or anything, it presents itself as this texture with the software on top. That is pretty common.

The other thing that would be vague is that I would just look at a range between very stark corporate, like Scale AI, and very interesting corporate or premium corporate, which would be Framer, and/or designery corporate, which would be Framer and Resend. I think that range is very important across the tech space because it will be their feel for who they want to go after, and the feel for their brand will fit into those buckets.

Again I would say that the layout itself needs to start with:

1. A sticky header.
2. Logo and branded navigation all the way across that collapses for mobile.
3. Main headline, subheadline, and call to action.
4. First main product tout.
5. Who the customers it serves.
6. The features of its platform or product, either in a horizontal tab situation or a left tab sidebar.
7. The ability to get the product.
8. Newsletter.
9. Footer.

The footer should, like these main tech sites, contain all the navigation, even more so than the top, and actually has your top navigation as well.

For this prompt the vague one should have the product's platform separated into:

- Platform.
- Resources.
- Developers.
- Pricing.

On the top right it would also have Sign In and Start for Free or Try for Free.

### Prompt 2

For the vague one the color palettes should range, with inspiration from traditional corporate SaaS through to premium, and then the middle there is getting a little bit more expressive depending upon your brand. As we slide through progressive we have serif fonts, then we possibly have some funkier fonts, and then by the time we get back to premium we're utilizing just Inter and very clean SF Pros or things like that.
