# HD-02 — Explicit Voice Prompt

## Brief

**Intent brief:** A startup wants a premium, modern tech/SaaS website with the polish of a leading tech brand.

**Category:** Editorial brand — tech/SaaS.

**Version:** Explicit — detailed research, reference roles, page flow, and art direction. The [vague version](HD-02-vague.md) records a separate, broader brief.

**Status:** Prompt two, in progress. We are drafting the prompt for a future code board. The first research round contains 36 modern tech, SaaS, and AI references. The direction spans corporate, organic, expressive, and premium. Attio, Granola, and Resend now provide full-page structure references for corporate, organic, and premium respectively. The expressive references remain Clay, Notion, and Browserbase. The final selection of variations and number of versions remain open.

## Sequence — how we developed the prompt

1. **Establish the category:** an editorial brand site for a premium, modern tech/SaaS startup.
2. **State the intent:** give the startup the polish of a leading tech brand. Keep this intent brief explicit as the prompt develops.
3. **Research the range:** collect 25–50 references, including Ramp, Cognition, Natural, and Linear. Open the sites on the right and retain their links. Prioritize the past year's launches and redesigns while distinguishing established references.
4. **Identify the endpoints:** Claude and Wispr Flow establish the organic, humanist end; Resend, Linear, and Framer establish the highly polished, dark, dimensional premium end.
5. **Define the expressive middle:** Clay adds playful, whimsical branding and a moldable, Play-Doh-like 3D character.
6. **Capture a reusable page structure:** Cohere provides the announcement strip, branded navigation, headline, subhead, and product showcase sequence.
7. **Identify a reusable imagery treatment:** float product imagery above soft blur or gradient textures, and carry that background treatment into tout cards and blog imagery.
8. **Confirm the shared SaaS structure:** Factory, Parallel, and Notion reinforce the sequence of anchored navigation, headline, supporting copy, primary action, and an initial product tout that invites scrolling.
9. **Expand the expressive treatments:** Browserbase introduces landscape imagery, dithering, dot matrix, and gradation; Notion introduces black-and-white brand illustration alongside Clay's moldable 3D.
10. **Distinguish the assistant layout:** when the product is an AI assistant, prioritize a headline, subhead, prominent input with a call to action, and suggested options beneath it. Replit is the reference for this focused entry experience.
11. **Expand the scale to four pillars:** corporate → organic → expressive → premium. This develops the earlier three-part range while retaining the earlier notes as a trace.
12. **Select a primary hero reference:** Resend supplies a premium composition with direct, static typography on the left, a simple dimensional object on the right, and a ray of light across the background. Changing the shape and typography can bring the same structure toward organic.
13. **Add an image-led email-entry variation:** Mercury sits near the corporate end, one or two steps toward expression. Explore an unusual image or video, optional parallax and progressive blur toward the top, with a simple email-entry call to action above the fold.
14. **Select the organic hero:** Granola pairs left-aligned humanist serif typography and the main action with three textured, layered planes on the right. The user describes this organic feel as approximately 3 on a 0–5 scale.
15. **Select the corporate anchor:** Attio provides the clean, centered SaaS introduction and the longer progression from customer proof to platform explanation, feature tour, renewed CTA, and a major differentiating statement.
16. **Map the complete scroll:** review Attio for corporate, Granola for organic, and Resend for premium through their closing calls to action and footers. Keep observed section order separate from proposed animation choices.

### Shared SaaS structure — Factory, Parallel, and Notion

Use this recurring hierarchy as a layout foundation:

1. Top navigation with logo and branding, anchored or sticky where appropriate.
2. A direct headline stating what the product does.
3. Supporting subhead.
4. A clear primary action: download, get the product, or start using it.
5. An initial product tout or showcase that introduces the product and invites scrolling.

- **[Factory](https://factory.com/):** header and logo, main statement, how to get or download the product, then the initial tout. It is also a reference for the high-end corporate side of premium.
- **[Parallel](https://parallel.ai/):** similar sticky header, headline, subhead, button, and main showcase, with a more technical or "nerdy" character.
- **[Notion](https://www.notion.com/):** top-anchored navigation, headline, supporting copy, buttons, and the main product showcase. Black-and-white illustration gives this familiar sequence an expressive brand character.

### Primary organic hero — Granola

Use Granola as the primary organic hero direction. Keep the navigation above a split composition: a left-aligned serif headline, supporting copy, and main interaction on the left; stacked, animated product imagery and textured planes on the right. The three planes described in the voice notes should feel layered and spread apart, giving the product a creative but human setting.

This combines a familiar SaaS proposition with a less technical, humanist presentation. Preserve that character through the full page, using the Granola flow below as the structural reference.

### Assistant-specific entry layout — Replit

When the SaaS product is an AI assistant, prioritize an **input-led hero** as a layout option suited to that product. Use [Replit](https://replit.com/) as the reference from the voice notes:

1. A clean, off-white surface.
2. Headline and supporting subhead.
3. A prominent input field with its primary call to action.
4. Suggested tasks or targeting options directly beneath the input.

Keep the initial view focused on starting an interaction with the assistant. This product-specific entry pattern can sit within the four-pillar brand range.

### Primary hero reference — Resend

Include a hero variation based on the composition described at [Resend](https://resend.com/) when the code board is developed:

- **Left:** direct typography that states exactly what the product is. "Email for Developers" is the reference for the clarity and brevity of the message.
- **Right:** an eye-catching 3D object with a simple shape and restrained detail.
- **Background:** a ray of light that creates atmosphere around the object.
- **Motion:** keep the headline static; it does not need rotating words or animation.
- **Header:** retain a clear top header; a sticky header is an option for the eventual variation.

This is a primary premium hero example. Explore different right-hand shapes and left-hand type treatments to carry the same composition toward a clean, organic expression as well.

### Near-corporate image and email-entry hero — Mercury

Use [Mercury](https://mercury.com/) as a reference for familiar top navigation paired with distinctive, unexpected imagery. Place this direction **one or two steps from corporate toward expression**, near the left of the range.

Explore a large background image or video with a simple foreground call to action. The image may be generated if it serves the direction. Optional parallax and soft progressive blur toward the top can connect the media to the navigation and text.

For a SaaS product whose first step is entering an email to open an account or launch the experience, use this as an above-the-fold variation: distinctive media, a concise message, an email field, and a clear action. Keep the email-entry purpose distinct from the assistant's task/prompt input.

*Video, parallax, generated imagery, and progressive blur are proposed explorations from the voice notes; the notes do not establish how Mercury's current imagery was produced or implemented.*

### Reusable product and card backgrounds

For variations that use textured backdrops, show the product **floating over a textured image background**. The texture should read as a soft blur, gradient, or atmospheric image, with the product remaining clear above it. Also allow the cleaner Attio treatment described in the voice notes, where the animated product itself leads without a separate textured card behind it.

Treat this as a reusable visual treatment across the page: the main product showcase, tout cards or feature buckets, and blog-post imagery can share this background language. The background creates depth and continuity across the brand's content.

Expand these backgrounds and touts with **dithering, dot matrix, and gradation explorations**. Browserbase is the expressive reference for a landscape with a dot-matrix character. Explore recognizable imagery such as a mountain landscape or horses translated into dithered treatments. These are proposed motifs for the visual range.

Keep the available treatments distinct enough to explore: soft atmospheric gradients, dithered landscape imagery, black-and-white illustration, and dimensional objects each offer a different route to brand expression.

### Page flow — above and below the fold

Use this as a planning framework, then adapt the order and depth to the product and the selected full-page reference:

| Stage | Role in the page |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Orientation | Optional announcement strip, then clear branding and navigation anchored at the top. |
| 2. Proposition and action | Headline, supporting explanation, and a primary action. Choose a centered stack, a split hero, an assistant input, or an email-entry composition. |
| 3. Product introduction | A main tout, product view, or dimensional hero object. Let it establish what the visitor is looking at and invite the next scroll. |
| 4. Credibility | Establish who uses the product. For a complex platform, early customer proof helps make the deeper explanation credible. |
| 5. Product explanation | State what the platform is, then explain what it does through a small set of understandable capabilities or workflow stages. |
| 6. Feature exploration | Pair short explanations with substantial product views. Horizontal selectors or a vertical left-side navigation can organize several features. |
| 7. Renewed action | Offer another opportunity to act after the visitor understands the product. |
| 8. Larger differentiating idea | Introduce a major statement or visual change that gives the longer page a fresh narrative moment. |
| 9. Supporting evidence | Select relevant integrations, customer stories, reliability evidence, or updates for the product being presented. |
| 10. Close | End with a clear action and an organized footer. |

**Motion remains a later art-direction decision.** Explore the main tout sliding, shifting, animating, or changing state as the visitor moves into the product explanation. Feature selectors can coordinate copy and product views. Exact triggers, timing, and scroll behavior remain open. Preserve Resend's direct, static hero headline in its premium variation.
